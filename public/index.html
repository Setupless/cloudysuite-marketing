Mail, calendar, and files on your own Cloudflare account.

CloudySuite is an open source workspace for teams of 2 to 100 people. One person deploys it to the organisation's Cloudflare account. Everyone else signs in with a passkey and uses Mail, Calendar, and Storage at addresses on your own domain.

Four apps that share one sign-in

Each app has its own address and its own switch, so you can turn one off and keep the mail provider you have. They share one directory, so a person you add once can be mailed, invited, and shared with everywhere.

Mail

mail.example.com

Instead of Gmail.

  • Conversations, attachments, and search that understands operators like from: and in:trash.
  • Shared mailboxes such as support@, and aliases on any of your domains.
  • Labels and rules that belong to the mailbox, so everyone who shares it sees the same ones.
  • Ten seconds to undo a send. The wait happens on the server, so closing the tab doesn't lose the message.
  • Spam filtering built in. It reads SPF, DKIM, and DMARC, learns from what people mark, and never rejects mail.
  • Installs as an app, with notifications and recent mail saved for reading offline.
  • Bring old mail across from an .mbox file, such as a Google Takeout export.

Calendar

calendar.example.com

Instead of Google Calendar.

  • Today opens on what's next, with who's coming and who hasn't replied.
  • Day, week, and month views, personal and team calendars, and repeating events.
  • Invite colleagues or anyone with an email address. Their replies come back through Mail and update the event.
  • Invitations that arrive in Mail show as a card you can answer from, with Join and Add to my calendar.
  • Import any .ics file.

Storage

storage.example.com

Instead of Google Drive.

  • Team folders with their own members, and My files for each person.
  • Share with a person or a team as Can view or Can edit. The server enforces Can view, so hiding a button is never the only protection.
  • Uploads resume after a dropped connection.
  • Search, Starred, Shared with me, and Trash.
  • Save a mail attachment straight into a folder.
  • When someone leaves, hand their files to a colleague.

Account

auth.example.com

Instead of Google Admin.

  • Passkeys first, with a password and an authenticator app as the fallback.
  • Admins can require two-step sign-in for everyone.
  • Reset a person's sign-in and hand them a one-time link. It is never emailed, because the only mailbox they have may be the one they can't open.
  • Invite people with a single-use link sent to their address.
  • Add more email domains and aliases.
  • Turn an app off for the whole organisation. Nothing is deleted, and turning it back on restores everything.

Set up in the browser, with nothing to install

You need a domain on Cloudflare and the Workers Paid plan. The setup wizard links to Cloudflare's guide for each if you don't have them yet.

  1. Click Deploy to Cloudflare. It copies the code to your GitHub or GitLab account and creates the Worker, its database, and its storage.
  2. Open the Worker's address. The setup wizard starts there.
  3. Paste an API token. The wizard's link opens Cloudflare's token form already filled in. The token stays in your browser tab and is never stored.
  4. Choose your domain. The wizard reads your DNS and recommends where the apps should live, with its reasons.
  5. Turn on mail. The wizard enables Email Routing and Email Sending and attaches the app addresses. Cloudflare adds the SPF, DKIM, and DMARC records.
  6. Create the first admin. Then invite your team from Account.

The wizard checks its own work

  • Each step reads the current state in Cloudflare, makes the change, and reads it again.
  • If the API can't finish a step, you get the exact action, a link to that dashboard page, and a Check again button. It never assumes a manual step worked.
  • It never deletes a DNS record, and it asks before replacing mail routing that's already in use.
  • Only someone with a token for the Worker's own account can finish setup, so a stranger who finds the address can't claim it.

Know this before you deploy

CloudySuite is new. Some things can't run on Workers, and some aren't built yet.

No desktop or phone mail apps

Apple Mail and Outlook need IMAP and SMTP servers, which can't run on Workers. Mail is a web app you can install instead.

Calendars don't sync to phones yet

Phone calendar apps sign in with a plain password, which CloudySuite doesn't allow yet.

Not end-to-end encrypted

Cloudflare encrypts your data at rest. Cloudflare and your admins can reach it, which is what lets search and rules run on the server.

Size and recipient caps

A message can be 25 MiB, attachments included, with up to 50 recipients.

Sending starts slow on a new account

Cloudflare gives new accounts a low daily sending limit and raises it over time. Email Sending is in public beta.

Updates are applied by hand

The Deploy button makes an independent copy. You apply a release with a few Git commands, and CloudySuite never changes itself.

No scheduled send or snooze

They aren't in the first release.

Open source under the AGPL

Read the code, run it, and change it. If you run a modified copy for other people, the AGPL asks you to share your changes. The README explains each product decision and the reason for it.
